## Overview

**Problem statement** Outdoor enthusiasts in Puerto Rico — trail builders, mountain bikers, hikers, and runners — have no centralized way to know the current conditions of local trails. Unpredictable tropical weather forces community members to contact multiple social media groups just to get a trail status update. This uncertainty leads to plans being changed or canceled last minute, and in more serious cases, exposes users to hazardous or impassable trail conditions.

**Opportunity** By creating a community-powered, mobile-first trail condition reporting app, Trail Scope can become the single source of truth for trail status in Puerto Rico — with the foundation to scale globally. Reducing friction in getting and sharing real-time trail information increases safety, improves the outdoor experience, and strengthens the local trail community.

**Context** This is a design challenge submission for Figma Config Makeathon, completed within a 2-week sprint.

## Users & audience

**Primary persona — the active outdoor enthusiast** Mountain bikers, hikers, trail runners, and trail builders based in Puerto Rico. They are mobile-first, active on social media, and currently rely on WhatsApp groups or Facebook to get trail updates. Their biggest pain points are uncertainty before heading out and wasted trips due to poor conditions.

**Secondary persona — the trail steward** Trail builders and maintainers who want to alert the community about hazards, closures, or recent work done on a trail. They are proactive contributors who care about trail safety and community trust.

**Geographic focus** Puerto Rico (launch scope) — designed with global scalability in mind.

**Accessibility considerations** WCAG 2.1 AA as the baseline target. Key considerations: sufficient color contrast for the green / yellow / red status system (do not rely on color alone), large touch targets for outdoor use with gloves or wet hands, legible typography in bright sunlight conditions.

## Core feature set

**Trail condition reporting** Users report trail status via one of two entry points:

- Scanning a QR/barcode placed at the trailhead
- Searching for a trail by name within the app

**Status system (3 tiers)**

- 🟢 Green — safe, trails are in good condition
- 🟡 Yellow — use caution, some trails may not be suitable
- 🔴 Red — all trails are unsuitable, do not ride/hike

**Report details (optional)** Users can add surface and hazard tags: dry, damp, muddy, downed trees, rocky, and other. A free-text note field is also available for additional context.

## Scope

**In scope**

- Trail condition reporting flow (QR scan + search)
- Green / yellow / red status submission
- Hazard and surface condition tagging
- Free-text note field
- Trail status feed / home screen
- Mobile-first responsive web design (375px primary)
- Figma prototype and case study
- User authentication / accounts

**Out of scope**

- Native iOS or Android app
- Real backend or live data integration
- Trail mapping or GPS routing
- Push notifications
- Multi-language support (for this sprint)

**Mobile-first breakpoints** 375px (primary) → 768px (tablet) → 1280px (desktop)

## Constraints & dependencies

**Technical constraints** Web only — no native app. Prototype will be built in Figma. No real data or backend required for this challenge sprint.

**Design constraints** 2-week timeline limits research and iteration depth. Design system will be created from scratch during or after the brief phase.

**Dependencies**

- Access to local trail community for research validation (optional but valuable)
- QR/barcode UX pattern research to inform the scan flow
- Figma + Contra challenge submission requirements and deadlines
